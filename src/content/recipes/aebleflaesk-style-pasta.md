## Steps

1. Boil the pasta in well-salted water until just al dente. Reserve 400–500 ml pasta water, then drain.
2. Fry the bacon in a large pan over medium until nicely browned and crisp. Remove to a plate. Leave 2–3 tbsp fat in the pan (pour off any extra).
3. In the bacon fat, cook the onions with a pinch of salt for 8–10 minutes until soft and golden. Add thyme and a lot of black pepper for the last minute.
4. Lower the heat. Stir in 250 g applesauce and 1 tbsp vinegar (or lemon juice). Add 150–250 ml pasta water a little at a time, stirring, until you have a smooth, glossy sauce. If it tastes too sweet, add more pepper, another ½–1 tbsp vinegar or lemon, and optionally ½ tsp Dijon mustard.
5. Optional: stir in 150–250 ml cream or 3–5 tbsp crème fraiche. Add a splash more pasta water if needed to keep the sauce loose.
6. Add the pasta and bacon back to the pan. Toss for 30–60 seconds, adding pasta water as needed until the sauce coats every piece.
7. Taste and adjust: more salt (careful—bacon is salty), pepper, or thyme for depth; more vinegar or lemon if too sweet; butter or cream if too sharp; more pasta water if too thick. Serve with chives or parsley if you like.

This dish is inspired by Danish æbleflæsk (apple and pork)—savory and peppery, not dessert-like. Jarred applesauce gives body and a gentle sweetness; vinegar, pepper, and optional mustard keep it firmly in dinner territory.

Reserve plenty of pasta water—you’ll use it to build the sauce and to loosen it when tossing. Start with 250 g applesauce and add more only if you want it sweeter. The optional cream or crème fraiche softens the sauce and makes it cling to the pasta even more.
