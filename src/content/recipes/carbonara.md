## Steps

1. Fill a 12-inch high-walled skillet two-thirds full with water (about 2 liters). Add 2 tsp kosher salt and bring to a boil.
2. Place guanciale in a cold 12-inch cast iron skillet. Heat over medium, stirring occasionally to ensure even browning. Cook 4-5 minutes or until fully cooked and fat is rendered.
3. Add spaghetti to the boiling water and cook until al dente per package instructions. Reserve 375 mL pasta cooking water, then drain and return pasta to the now empty high-walled skillet. If guanciale is still cooking, drizzle a little olive oil over the pasta and cover to keep warm.
4. In a high-powered blender, combine grated cheese and egg yolks. With the blender running, gradually add 125 mL warm pasta cooking water and 60 mL rendered guanciale fat. Optionally add a piece of cooked guanciale for extra flavor. Blend 10-15 seconds until the sauce is smooth and glossy.
5. Transfer the cooked pasta to the skillet with the guanciale and any remaining fat. Stir over low heat with metal tongs.
6. Turn off the heat. Slowly pour the egg mixture over the pasta, stirring constantly with the tongs so the eggs don’t scramble.
7. Season with more kosher salt and black pepper to taste. Serve immediately.

![Carbonara sauce being tossed with spaghetti](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1551183053-bf91a1d81141?w=600&q=80)

Authentic carbonara gets its creaminess from egg yolks and starchy pasta water, not cream. The key is to work off the heat when you add the egg mixture and keep tossing so it emulsifies instead of scrambling.

Guanciale (cured pork jowl) is traditional, but bacon works well if you can’t find it. Use the rendered fat in the sauce for maximum flavor. Reserve plenty of pasta water—you may need a little extra to loosen the sauce when combining. Serve right away; it doesn’t hold well.
