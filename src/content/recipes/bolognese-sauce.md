## Steps

1. Dice all vegetables coarsely. Use a food processor to pulse carrots until finely coarse, mimicking ground beef. Do the same for other vegetables, blending garlic and onions together. Transfer to a bowl.
2. In a heavy pot, heat olive oil over medium-high. Add vegetables, season with salt and pepper, and cook for about 20 minutes until moisture is reduced and browning starts.
3. Add the ground beef, breaking it apart and seasoning well. Cook until browned and fond forms on the pot's bottom. Stir in tomato paste and cook for another 3-5 minutes, watching for burning.
4. Deglaze with red wine, scraping the bottom to loosen fond. Reduce wine by half, then add bay leaves, thyme, cheese rind, and enough water to cover the meat slightly. Simmer for 3.5-4 hours, adding water as needed if it evaporates.
5. Boil a large pot of salted water and cook pasta to 80% done.
6. Remove herbs, stir in cream and parmesan. Drain pasta and combine with the sauce over low heat. Adjust with pasta water and additional cheese until pasta is al dente.
7. Plate with extra sauce, topped with more cheese and chopped parsley.

This is a slow-cooked, traditional bolognese that requires patience but delivers incredible depth of flavor. The long simmering time allows the meat to break down and the flavors to meld together beautifully.

The key to great bolognese is the slow cooking—don't rush it. The sauce should be thick and rich by the end. Using a food processor to finely chop the vegetables helps them break down during cooking and creates a smoother texture. The cream and parmesan added at the end add richness and help bind the sauce to the pasta.

This sauce freezes beautifully and actually improves after a day or two in the fridge, making it perfect for meal prep.
