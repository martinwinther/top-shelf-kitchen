## Steps

1. Follow the pack instructions for noodles. If they're soak-only: soak in hot water until just flexible (still a bit firm), then drain.
2. Stir tamarind, fish sauce, sugar, soy sauce, and water until smooth-ish (tamarind can be lumpy—that's fine).
3. Heat a large pan or wok with oil. Fry chicken strips until browned and cooked through. Remove to a plate.
4. Add a touch more oil. Crack in eggs and scramble quickly. Push to the side.
5. Add onion and garlic, cook 30-60 seconds, then add cabbage and carrot. Stir-fry 2-3 minutes (keep some crunch).
6. Add drained noodles and chicken back in. Pour sauce over. Toss and fry hard 1-2 minutes until noodles are coated and slightly sticky. If it looks dry: splash in 1-2 tbsp water and keep tossing.
7. Plate it, then top with peanuts and coriander. Serve with lime wedges to squeeze over.

![Pad Thai being stir-fried in a wok](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1626808642875-0aa545482dfb?w=600&q=80)

This classic Pad Thai balances sweet, sour, and savory flavors. The key is getting the noodles to the right texture—they should be flexible but still have a slight bite before cooking.

The tamarind paste gives the signature tangy flavor. Start with 3 tbsp and adjust to your taste—you can always add more. Don't overcook the vegetables; they should retain some crunch for texture contrast. If the noodles seem dry during cooking, don't hesitate to add a splash of water—the sauce should coat everything evenly.

Serve immediately while hot, with extra lime wedges for squeezing over at the table.
